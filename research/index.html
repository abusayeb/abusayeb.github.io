Research statement

3 paragraphs

My research is computer vision, and the question that runs through it is whether a model's output can be trusted where it is actually used. I have come at that from several directions: uncertainty fusion for skin-lesion screening, physics-informed invariants for detecting AI-generated images, eye-disease screening and rice-leaf classification for low-resource settings, and, in 2026, perception for home service robots in scanned real homes.

Industry gave the same question a deadline. At Othello in Japan I built multi-object tracking, pose estimation and activity recognition on live video, where a model has to hold up against messy data and a fixed latency budget. That experience shaped how I run research now: the robot studies were preregistered, evaluated in the closed loop rather than by accuracy alone, and one of them is reported as the negative result it turned out to be.

For a Master's I am open to several of these threads rather than one: reliable and uncertainty-aware vision, video understanding, robot and embodied perception, and generative models. What I want from any of them is the same — methods that stay dependable under distribution shift and real deployment constraints, tested honestly.

RGEF — knowing when not to answer in skin-lesion screening

2026
First author · method, experiments and paper

A skin-lesion classifier that flags images it should not answer. RGEF fuses eight uncertainty signals — ensemble disagreement, evidential vacuity, conformal set size and five feature-space detectors — with a learned per-image reliability gate, accumulated as subjective-logic evidence.

⚡IEEE RAAICON 2026 (in press) · journal extension under review at BSPC
Case study

Problem

Deep classifiers for skin-lesion diagnosis return a confident label even for lesion types they were never trained on, and the usual fix — fusing uncertainty signals with one fixed weighting — treats every image the same.

Approach

  • Gate conditioned on frozen self-supervised (DINOv2) features rather than the task backbone, giving each signal its own reliability per image.
  • The journal version filters the external archive against the training corpus by identifier and pixel hashing, removing 10,015 training-derived images before evaluation.
  • In the journal version, on the remaining 14,602 in-distribution and 666 out-of-distribution images, gated fusion reaches AUROC 0.7946 under acquisition shift, against 0.6945 for static fusion.
  • Reported honestly where it struggles: in the conference version, on smartphone images (PAD-UFES-20), the gain over simpler fusion is small.

Experiment

Conference version: trained on HAM10000 with two lesion classes held out as novel, then evaluated zero-shot on ISIC 2019 and PAD-UFES-20 against eleven published out-of-distribution detectors and static fusion. The journal version re-runs the external test on the deduplicated archive.

Contribution

The reliability thread in my work: a model that can say when its own reading should not be trusted.

Learning When to Look — adaptive perception for home robots

2026
First author · design, build and analysis

A preregistered closed-loop test of whether a navigating home robot can save detector compute by learning when to run it, where to look and which model size to use. Built in Habitat on scanned HM3D homes, with obstacles that reach the planner only through the detector.

⚡85% less detector compute · success criterion not met · under review at Intelligent Service Robotics
Case study

Problem

Adaptive perception is a popular way to cut a robot's compute, but it is rarely tested where a missed detection costs a collision or an unfinished task.

Approach

  • Controller learns when to run the detector, with hand-designed rules for where to look and which model size to use, a safety shield and an experience memory.
  • Navigation stack made to work in real-home scans: a static-map costmap layer, visibility-aware track ageing and floor-grounded obstacles took no-obstacle success from 0.55 to 0.95.
  • Task, validity gates and success criterion fixed in a preregistration; the comparison against twelve baselines was run once, with 120 paired episodes per policy in 25 held-out homes.
  • Reported numbers come only from a certification pipeline that checks code, data and provenance before a result can enter the paper.

Experiment

The learned scheduler cut computation by 85% relative to running the heavy detector on every frame, but reached the goal in 55.0% of episodes against 79.2%, and collided in 8.3% against 0.8% for the light detector on every frame. The preregistered criterion was not met, and the paper reports it that way.

Contribution

My first robotics study, and a negative result I am glad to have: for a home robot, running the cheapest adequate detector on every frame is a strong default to beat.

HomeClean — doorways and robot vacuum cleaning

2026
First author · design, build and analysis

A parameter-free room-graph model that predicts how long a robot vacuum takes to clean a home from its floor plan, and where two robots obstruct each other. Tested in a simulation benchmark of 200 scanned homes with clutter, five cleaning strategies and two-robot teams.

⚡17,580 simulated runs · R² 0.81 on 100 unseen homes · under review at Robotics and Autonomous Systems
Case study

Problem

Nobody can yet say from a floor plan how long a randomly moving robot vacuum will take, or where two robots in the same home will get in each other's way.

Approach

  • Turns a home's geometry into a Markov chain over rooms: exit rates from a billiard door law, per-point cleaning rates from Crofton's formula, no fitted parameters.
  • First preregistered study on 100 homes: the door law held (slope 0.86) and the model ranked homes well, but predicted cleaning faster than simulated.
  • Diagnosed the gap — clutter makes visits to a spot come in bursts — and fixed it with two corrections from the same billiard picture.
  • A second preregistration, frozen before 100 unseen homes were downloaded, confirmed the refined model, replicated the door law and showed a vacuum and a delivery robot jam at the doors delivery routes use most.

Experiment

Five cleaning strategies and two-robot teams were simulated in 200 scanned homes (17,580 runs). On the 100 unseen homes the refined model reached R² 0.81, near the ceiling set by run-to-run variation. It describes robots that bounce; for Roomba-like robots it ranks homes but misjudges absolute times.

Contribution

Theory checked against a large simulated benchmark, twice preregistered, with the failed predictions reported next to the ones that held.

It drives the portal through Playwright — login, form filling, encryption and signing, document mapping and a verification pass — and keeps going through crashes, network drops and half-finished runs.

⚡First startup product · v2.2.0, shipping on macOS and Windows
Case study

Problem

Preparing a tender on the national e-GP portal is long, repetitive and unforgiving: one unfilled form or unmapped document can invalidate a bid, and the work has to be redone from the start if the connection drops.

Approach

Contribution

06 — Proposed Master’s research

Proposed Master’s research

What I want to investigate during a Master’s — one question for each of the threads above that I most want to take further. Each extends something I have already built rather than starting from scratch.

5 questions
Reliable & uncertainty-aware AI

Can a screening model tell a new camera from a new disease?

RGEF flags inputs it should not answer, but a single uncertainty score mixes two very different causes: an image from an unfamiliar device or clinic (acquisition shift) and a lesion type the model has never seen (semantic novelty). The first calls for recalibration; the second for a specialist. Conditioning the gate on frozen self-supervised features is a first step toward separating them, and the conference version's weakest dataset, smartphone images in PAD-UFES-20, shows how far there is to go. I want to make that separation explicit and test it across institutions.

Video understanding

Does activity recognition survive the move from a fixed camera to one that moves?

Everything I have built for tracking, activity recognition and pose estimation assumed a camera that stays put: ByteTrack, MoveNet and MMAction2 running on live retail video, with OPTICS clustering on top of the pose output. A robot carries its own camera, so ego-motion, a shifting viewpoint and self-occlusion all arrive at once. I want to know how much of that pipeline transfers and which parts have to be rebuilt rather than retuned. The closest thing I have to a first answer is the Universal NFT work, where models tuned on one controlled setting had to hold up on unconstrained user video.

Robot perception & navigation

Can a model's own uncertainty drive a robot's actions, not just flag them?

RGEF and its journal extension stop at flagging a single decision. My adaptive-perception study showed what happens when a robot's choice of when to look is not tied to how sure it is: the misses turned into collisions. The extension I want to work on is feeding that evidential state into the control loop directly — slowing down, looking again or handing off to a human, in real time rather than as an offline diagnostic.

Generative models & image forensics

What perceptual structure survives when the sensor or hardware changes?

The forensic invariant work looked for cues that persist across different image generators. The same question applies to a robot or embedded system moving between cameras, lighting conditions and constrained onboard compute. I want to know how far that invariant-seeking approach carries over from detecting synthetic images to holding perception steady on real hardware in the field.

Edge AI & efficient perception

Models are advancing far faster than the hardware meant to run them. What gives first?

My closed-loop study gave a first, uncomfortable answer: safety goes first. A robot that learned when to run its detector saved 85% of the compute but reached its goal less often and collided more than one that simply ran a small detector on every frame. At Othello the same squeeze showed up as getting models onto edge devices at all, where parallel inference bought a ~70% latency cut only by trading complexity against a fixed budget. I want to find the conditions under which skipping perception is actually safe, and test them on real embedded hardware rather than in simulation.
